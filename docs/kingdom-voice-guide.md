# Kingdom Voice Guide — Mwata Kazembe Kingdom Website

**Prepared:** 2026-05-31  
**Authority:** For all editors, contributors, and developers working on the official digital home of the Kingdom of Kazembe  
**Purpose:** Ensure every word on the site speaks from the Kingdom's seat of authority, not as an outsider reporter, tourist guide, or academic describing the Kingdom from the outside

---

## A. Voice Principles

### 1. Speak from authority
The Kingdom does not ask for permission to exist. It does not explain itself apologetically. It presents its history, office, ceremony, and people with the confidence of an institution that has governed, migrated, traded, and survived for over 280 years.

### 2. Speak from continuity
The Kingdom of Kazembe is a living institution, not a historical relic. Every section should reflect that the royal seat at Mwansabombwe is active today — not that it was once active in the past.

### 3. Speak as ourselves
This is our site, not a report about us. On the public pages the Kingdom speaks in the first person: **we** and **our**, not "the Kingdom" held at arm's length. "We crossed the Luapula at Matanda." "Our court sits twenty-six seats." "We did not fall in those years."

Individual voices (the editor, the developer, the researcher) stay invisible: "we" is the Kingdom, never the people building the site. Avoid the hedging first person — "we think", "we hope", "we are trying", "we believe" — which weakens the voice rather than carrying it.

The Mwata is not "we". He is spoken of as He, with the third person and the capital, and the offices of the Kingdom are named as they are. Registry entries, image credits, alt text, and source notes stay descriptive; they are records, not the Kingdom speaking.

### 4. Speak with cultural respect
The language of the site must honour the Lunda-Kazembe heritage without exoticising it. Ceremony is ceremony — not a festival or tourist attraction. The Mwata is the Mwata — not a "chief" or a "leader" in generic terms.

### 5. Avoid academic distance
Unless quoting a source directly in the attribution section, avoid language that sounds like a dissertation: "it has been argued that", "scholars have suggested", "there is evidence to suggest". On public pages, state what is known.

### 6. Avoid apology for data gaps
Where information is not yet confirmed, the page should not explain the gap publicly. Instead: either present what is known with confidence, or leave the section clean without explanation. The gap is documented internally in `content-verification-needed.md`.

### 7. Preserve factual discipline
Do not invent. Do not fill gaps with speculation. If a fact is uncertain, the internal confidence label handles this (used in the lineage rail and timeline). Public-facing text presents only what is supportable.

## C. Banned Patterns

The following phrases must never appear on public pages:

| Banned | Reason |
|---|---|
| "This website is about…" | Sounds like a school project |
| "Here you can learn about…" | Generic tourism language |
| "The Kingdom may have…" | Exposes uncertainty without authority |
| "We are trying to preserve…" | Apologetic; implies failure or struggle |
| "This page gives information…" | Generic — not institutional |
| "Credit pending confirmation" | Exposes an internal gap as a public headline |
| "Tentative until official confirmation" | Implies the Kingdom is unsure of its own ceremony dates |
| "To be confirmed by the Royal Office" | Exposes internal workflow publicly |
| "Will be published once confirmed" | Forward-tense gap exposure |
| "Under review" | Implies dysfunction |
| "Forthcoming" | Weak placeholder as headline |
| "Pending publication" | Shows as a date or headline — looks incomplete |
| "It is believed that…" | Academic hedge — not institutional voice |
| "Some sources say…" | Outsider framing |
| "According to press reports…" | Not appropriate for first-person institutional voice |
| "Explore our little site…" | Diminishing language |
| "We hope you enjoy…" | Tourist pamphlet language |
| "Officer list to be published" | Exposes governance gap publicly |
| "Awaiting Royal Office" | Shows in public table cells — deeply weak |
| "Verified register pending" | Table cell visible to all visitors — apologetic |
| "once contact channels are confirmed" | Implies the institution has no working contact |

## D. Strong Replacement Patterns

### Gap in institutional data
**Instead of:** "Current membership and officers are to be published by the Royal Office."  
**Write:** "The Royal Council represents the Kingdom's senior advisory structure, carrying deliberation and coordinating authority across Luapula."

### Uncertain ceremony date
**Instead of:** "Expected 24–25 July 2026 (Tentative until official confirmation)"  
**Write:** "Expected 24–25 July 2026 — confirm with the Royal Protocol Office before travel."

### Missing contact email
**Instead of:** "Direct contact to be confirmed by the Protocol Office."  
**Write:** "Direct inquiries to the Royal Protocol Office, Mwansabombwe."

### Incomplete biography
**Instead of:** "Further biographical detail is to be published by the Office of the Mwata when officially released."  
**Write:** [Present what is confirmed. Do not explain the gap.]

### Pending news
**Instead of:** "Kingdom news — pending publication"  
**Write:** "Kingdom Newsroom — Updates and Announcements"

### Clan register not yet published
**Instead of:** Table showing "Verified register pending" and "Awaiting Royal Office" in cells  
**Write:** [Hide the empty table. Show the authoritative editorial note instead.]

### Publication forthcoming
**Instead of:** "Official kingdom publications — forthcoming"  
**Write:** "Royal Gazette and Official Publications"

## F. Writing the Mwata

The Mwata Kazembe is the supreme traditional authority of the Kingdom. Writing about the office requires care:

**Do:**
- Present the office, the reign, the ceremonial role, and the governance function using documented facts
- Use the full title: "His Royal Highness Mwata Kazembe XIX"
- Reference the royal seat: "from Mwansabombwe"
- Describe function: "presides over Umutomboko", "carries the duties of leadership and ceremonial authority"

**Do not:**
- Write celebrity biography ("He was born in...")
- Overclaim personal achievements without verification
- Reduce the office to a single sentence ("the Mwata is a traditional leader")
- Expose any gaps in the biography text publicly

## G. Writing Ceremony

Umutomboko / Mutomboko is a state ceremony of memory, identity, conquest, and renewal. It is not a festival. It is not a tourist attraction. It is not simply a dance.

**Strong framing:**
- "The state ceremony of Umutomboko is held at Mwansabombwe in the last weekend of July."
- "Mutomboko stands as one of the defining ceremonies of the Lunda-Kazembe people."
- "Through rhythm, procession, gathering, and allegiance, the ceremony affirms the continuity of the Kingdom."

**Avoid:**
- "The Mutomboko festival is a popular event"
- "The dance entertains visitors"
- "This colorful tradition is enjoyed by…"

## H. Writing History

The history of the Kingdom of Kazembe is real, complex, and well-sourced in academic and archival records. The site does not need to hedge everything with "it is believed" or "oral tradition suggests".

**Approach:**
- State what is established: migration from Kola, settlement in Luapula, trade with Portuguese/Arab networks, 19 recorded rulers
- Where dating is uncertain, the internal confidence label on the lineage rail handles this — do not repeat the hedge in the main narrative
- Write history as movement, settlement, authority, and continuity — not as external events that happened to the Kingdom

**Strong framing:**
- "The Kingdom of Kazembe traces its founding to Ng'anga Bilonda and the Lunda migration from Kola toward the Luapula Valley."
- "Kazembe authority grew through tribute networks, diplomatic encounter, and military consolidation across the Luapula-Mweru corridor."
- "The Kingdom survived colonial pressure, rebuilding its seat at Mwansabombwe through institutional resilience and cultural continuity."
